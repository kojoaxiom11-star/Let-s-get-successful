You're not behind — nobody goes from a BSc straight to "gene editing scientist." Everyone in these fields follows some version of this staircase. This is just here so the dream has a shape, not just weight.
Now — Year(s) in BSc Agric. Biotech
Build the real foundation
Lean into genetics, molecular biology & statistics electives — these matter more for your dream than the "agric" parts. Get comfortable with lab technique: PCR, sequencing, cell culture.
Alongside classes, ongoing
Self-teach bioinformatics
Your degree won't cover this deeply, but it's learnable on your own: Python or R, basic stats, Coursera's Bioinformatics Specialization, practice problems on Rosalind.info. A few hours a week compounds fast.
Before graduation
Get real research/lab experience
Ask a lecturer if you can assist with genetics or molecular research — even unpaid. This matters more than grades alone for grad school or forensic/genetics jobs.
Then it branches — pick based on what pulls you most
Postgrad · Gene editing
MSc/PhD in Molecular Biology, Genetics, or Biotechnology
Straight continuation of your current path, deepened. CRISPR and gene-editing work almost always sits inside a research lab at this level.
Postgrad · Bioinformatician
MSc in Bioinformatics or Computational Biology
Many programs accept a biology background plus self-taught coding — which is exactly what you'll already have if you keep at the Python/R practice now.
Postgrad · Forensic / Criminal DNA expert
MSc in Forensic Science or Forensic Genetics
A distinct specialization — worth researching specific programs early, including options in Ghana and abroad, since not every university offers this directly.
6–10 years out — normal, not slow
Working scientist / expert
This timeline is standard for everyone in these fields, not a sign you're behind. Every step you're taking now — even a single revision session — is already part of this staircase.
